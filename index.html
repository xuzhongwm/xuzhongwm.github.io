Undergraduate Honors Thesis

List-Recoloring and the Diameter of Reconfiguration Graphs

Mar 2025 – May 2026

Alongside the machine learning work, I spent March 2025 through May 2026 on graph theory as my undergraduate honors thesis at William & Mary under Prof. Daniel W. Cranston and Prof. Gexin Yu. The work asks: given two proper colorings of a graph, how many single-vertex steps are needed to get from one to the other?

  • Generalized Extension Lemma — strengthens the Extension Lemma by bounding recoloring sequences over arbitrary subgraphs rather than a single vertex.
  • Global discharging argument — exploits global graph structure to reach sharper density bounds than classical local discharging allows.
  • 5-list recoloring has linear diameter for mad(G) < 3 − ε, sharp relative to Cereceda's conjecture.
  • The linear bound for 6-list recoloring improves from 3.4 to 3.75 − ε.
  • Presented as a poster at the Joint Mathematics Meetings, Washington, D.C., January 2026.
